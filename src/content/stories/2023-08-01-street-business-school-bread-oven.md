The Street Business School program implemented in Burundi by Transform International (TI) teaches entrepreneurial skills to men and women. It is having an enormously positive impact on the men and women who graduate: they are doubling and tripling their own incomes and generating employment for themselves and others in their communities.

Burundi is an underdeveloped country of 13 million people that has suffered a troubled history of civil war and insecurity. It is the poorest country in the world. (For perspective, the GDP per capita in Burundi is less than half that of Afghanistan.) The Trauma Healing and Reconciliation Services (THARS) center is in Burundi’s second city, Gitega. THARS provides a wide range of rehabilitation services to direct and indirect victims of trauma to help them reintegrate into society.

THARS, in partnership with Transform International Canada (TIC), determined that a program focused on developing entrepreneurial and business skills would benefit THARS clientele, since most people attending THARS have very little in the way of any education. Implementation at THARS was in recognition that economic self-sufficiency forms one part of overall rehabilitation after trauma. The Street Business School (SBS) curriculum was chosen by THARS and TIC for its excellent track record elsewhere in Africa. The SBS program aims to help people improve their economic resilience and situation by teaching them practical entrepreneurial skills.

## Doubling incomes

The SBS program currently has its third cohort of 20 students in house; they are set to graduate in August 2023. A fourth cohort of 30 students will begin their studies in August or September 2023.

Data gathered from graduates thus far shows that on average, students are doubling their income (from 3 USD per day to 6 USD) within months of starting the program. It is an understatement to say this is having a tremendously positive effect on the lives of the students. It is also creating a “virtuous circle” for the local communities: SBS graduates are starting small businesses and providing much-needed economic life to impoverished villages.

## A rocket oven for bread

THARS also provides a lunch program (go to school, receive a free lunch) for local Twa children, who are marginalized. In partnership with TIC and Ngozi University, a specially designed commercial bread “Rocket Oven” was built at THARS last year.

- The bread oven will immediately begin to save THARS money, as it will no longer have to purchase bread on the open market, allowing it to expand its lunch program.
- Second, the oven will have an entrepreneurial aspect, as SBS students will eventually learn how to operate a bread-baking business.
- Lastly, the rocket oven design uses 90% less wood than a conventional oven, meaning that it will help to reduce deforestation, which is already widespread in Burundi.

Plans are being developed to build a second rocket oven at a new location, with a view to introducing this environmentally friendlier technique throughout Burundi. Exciting times ahead!
