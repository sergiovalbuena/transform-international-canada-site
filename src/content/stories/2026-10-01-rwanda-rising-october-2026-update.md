I am awed by the ever-increasing success rate of our students. They say they are inspired to diligence by the scholarship winners who have preceded them and then, they become the inspiration for those who follow them.

We promote attendance and alertness at school by providing daily lunch to 285 kids who used to go without. University students have almost doubled to 45 in two years.

Eighty struggling moms are getting help to provide the food and secure home that will keep their kids off the streets. You may sense the momentum for good that has been built up and promises to expand in the changed lives of our beneficiaries. They say they will not forget, and I see them live this in their many acts of kindness toward others.

## The journey to university

Nothing compares with the proud, joyful faces of youth just completing a long, arduous challenge, especially if they began it as underdogs.

Three years later six or seven of them come again, proud and joyful, having earned university tuition scholarships — at three times the national rate — and asking only for a modest living stipend of $100 a month, $1,200 a year (an increase based on 15% inflation), to realize an impossible dream.

We now sponsor 45 in university. They study hard, live frugally, and graduate as teachers, nurses, lab technicians, accountants, engineers, doctors. Virtually all are employed in two months and sending money home.

They come from adobe houses with leaky tin roofs, no water or electricity; with one meal a day from a single mom; where sharing and determination become their code.

They graduated grade 9 in the top 30% of their class, earning invitations to three-year boarding schools. The $360 a year tuition is impossible for their families and so, they appealed to Rwanda Rising for a scholarship. Eighteen are accepted each year and eighteen graduate, for a total of 54 sponsored in boarding.

This is a value chain of family motivation, student determination, our sponsorship and a quality education that produces mature adults, forever grateful and committed to lifting others.

Their semesters began in September, so now is the time they are asking for your support.

We are deeply grateful to the Zoie Gardner Overseas Fund of the Unitarian Universalist Community of Victoria for its generous support of education for girls from poor families. This legacy will last for generations.

## A vital hand up for families

The **Street Kids Project**, launched in March this year, has brought 50 truant kids back to regular attendance at school.

For a year we are providing daily lunch, uniforms and supplies. We reorient them through weekend programs in sports, the arts and discussions about their choices and direction in life. Most have become model students and now contribute their efforts to improving an impoverished home life.

The **Women in Transition Project**, launched in August, is empowering 30 women to leave the sex trade while learning new skills.

They had come to a parish priest asking for a new way to feed their children. A $7,000 grant from my Oak Bay Rotary Club is building their solidarity and their earning capacity to feed their children.

We are providing a training program in handcraft production for income, a supplemental food supply for six months and a three-year lease on land that will produce three harvests a year — food for families and saving for the next planting. They now have a bond, a skill and a confidence with which they will lift themselves out of disrepute and desperation.

We have visited all their homes to assess what can be done about the deplorable conditions in which these single mothers, who earn $10 a week for field work, are raising an average of three kids each.

I need donations of up to $2,000 to buy blankets, mosquito nets, pots, fix leaking roofs, install doors, provide chickens and group gardens. Their hopes to feed and encourage their kids to rise above hopelessness are like our hopes, only much more desperate. Your support will go a long way to keep these moms on their mission.
