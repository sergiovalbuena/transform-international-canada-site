**72 new entrepreneurs. One graduation. Dozens of lives changed.**

The Street Business School (SBS) program in Gitega, Burundi, has made a strong start to 2026. The highlight of the first quarter was the graduation of cohorts 7, 8 and 9 on January 30, 2026, where 72 new entrepreneurs successfully completed the program and launched their own income-generating activities.

These graduates join a growing network of small business owners who are transforming not only their own lives, but also the wellbeing of their families and communities. As we have consistently seen, the impact is immediate and tangible: improved food security, children returning to school, and local economic activity driven by newly created businesses.

## A productive quarter on the ground

- **Exit coaching for cohort 9** was conducted in January, with 22 of 25 participants visited at their businesses. This allowed us to collect final data and observe firsthand the early results of their training.
- The **graduation ceremony** for cohorts 7, 8 and 9 took place on January 30, bringing together participants, local leaders and community members. The event featured testimonials, speeches and celebration — reinforcing both accountability and pride among graduates.
- In March, the team undertook **identification and recruitment activities** across multiple hills in Bugendana, engaging over 170 potential participants and selecting **64 new entrepreneurs** for cohorts 10 and 11. After much discussion, THARS is now starting cohorts with more than 30 students, so that after some inevitably drop out for various life reasons (sickness, family reasons and so on), we can still aim to graduate close to 30 students per cohort.
- **Baseline coaching visits** were completed for all new participants, ensuring that we have accurate intake data and that each participant begins the program with a clear understanding of their business goals.

All planned activities for the quarter were successfully completed — a strong indicator of program stability and execution capacity.

## Impact and results

Participants consistently report that the program equips them not only with business skills, but with the confidence and mindset needed to succeed. Many shift from struggling or failing businesses to viable, growing enterprises – in other words, they become economically resilient and self-sufficient – which is our immediate goal. The greater good is the consequence of greater incomes: food stability and education of the next generation. When a parent succeeds as a result of being educated in our Street Business School, they are more likely to be able to pay the school fees and educate their children. In other words, each graduate from our program results in a number of children going to school as well!

One participant shared how she closed an unprofitable restaurant after realizing it was not suited to her local market, and successfully transitioned into selling fruits and vegetables — a business aligned with community demand.

These are not isolated examples. Happily, they reflect a consistent pattern for our graduates:

- Participants start and sustain businesses
- Household incomes increase
- Families become more food secure
- Graduates begin to create jobs for others
- Children attend school

In short, this is the Street Business School “virtuous circle” in action. I continue to work with THARS to think of ways to grow our program and make it more efficient and at least partially self-sustaining. We are tiny in the big picture, but there is no doubt in my mind that we are changing lives at the coal face in the villages around Gitega, where our students come from.

## Student profile: Niyonkuru Richard, from struggling vendor to job creator

Niyonkuru Richard, age 25, from Bugendana Commune, is a graduate of cohort 7 and an excellent example of the transformative impact of Street Business School.

Before joining the program, Richard operated a small shop and sold doughnuts, but lacked the business skills and capital needed to succeed. Despite his ambition, his ventures repeatedly failed.

Through SBS training, Richard developed a new approach to entrepreneurship — learning how to identify profitable opportunities, manage capital, and start small but strategically. He made the bold decision to close his shop, which was valued at 500,000 Burundian francs (approximately $170 USD / ~$230 CAD), and reinvest in a new venture.

Today, Richard operates a successful bakery, launched with an initial investment of 1,500,000 Burundian francs (approximately $510 USD / ~$690 CAD). Within six months, the business has grown significantly, generating strong revenues and allowing him to **employ ten people** from his community.

This growth has not only transformed Richard’s own economic prospects, but has also created income opportunities for others, enabling multiple families to better support themselves. His story is a powerful example of how SBS drives both individual and community-level impact.

## Challenges

While all planned activities were completed this quarter, several ongoing challenges continue to affect program delivery:

- **Fuel shortages** remain a persistent issue, making travel to remote communities more difficult and increasing operational complexity. Burundi lacks the hard currency required to import sufficient fuel, and higher oil prices will worsen its ability to import enough fuel to power the country. It is a crisis that affects the economy and daily life of Burundians, and Street Business School is no exception. THARS (like everybody else) has developed work-arounds to cope as best as possible, but unfortunately, there is little hope for improvement in the short run.
- **Logistical constraints** in rural areas (no internet, no cell service, no fuel) continue to require flexibility and careful planning from the team, notably our two trainers, Elie and Sosthene, who have been with us since day one of the program.

Despite these challenges, THARS and Transform International (Canada) successfully achieved all program objectives for the quarter.

## Looking ahead

With all first-quarter activities completed as planned, the program is well positioned for the rest of 2026. Our objectives for the year are to:

- **Complete four full cohorts in Gitega**, for the first time ever, and
- **Expand to a second school in Muyinga** (Ngozi region), in partnership with THARS.

Initial groundwork for expansion is underway, and we are exploring opportunities to collaborate with local stakeholders, including the Ngozi Rotary Club, as part of a broader strategy to scale sustainably.

Street Business School continues to deliver measurable, life-changing impact in one of the world’s most challenging environments. The graduation of three cohorts, combined with the immediate launch of two new cohorts, reflects both the strength of the model and the dedication of our team and participants.
