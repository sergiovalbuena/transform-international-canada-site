      - Open Hands Education Trust (OHET) is a community-based charity serving the rural village of Dadawan and surrounding areas in Bihar State, northeastern India. It was founded by Jhulan Kumar. Since 2017, OHET has worked to improve quality of life and bring hope to families living in deep, multi-generational poverty through practical, community-driven programs.
      - In 2024, OHET became a registered charitable trust in India. In 2025 it began a partnership with TIC to enhance its charitable work.
      - Together, OHET and TIC support children, women, older adults and other vulnerable community members as they build healthier and more secure futures. Through education, vocational training, community health initiatives and practical assistance, the partnership helps break the cycle of extreme poverty and strengthens individuals to reach their full potential.
      - Five programs are offered through OHET with TIC support.

  job-skills:
    type: program
    id: job-skills
    number: 1
    heading: Job Skills Program for *Rural Women*
    body:
      - Since 2017, OHET has operated a six-month sewing and tailoring program that equips women with vocational skills for employment or starting a home-based business. In 2025, the program relocated from Dadawan village to the nearby town of Goh, making it more accessible to women from surrounding villages. About 60 women train each year, in classes of approximately 30 that begin at different times.
      - Graduates receive certificates that enhance work opportunities, and financial assistance toward purchasing a treadle sewing machine and other basic equipment needed to earn an income from home. Treadle machines are essential because electricity is unreliable or unavailable in many villages.
      - Their new livelihoods help them support their families, provide nutritious food and keep their children in school. Beyond vocational skills, these participants gain confidence, emotional resilience and self-worth.
    outcomesLead: Graduates in Goh are already employed
    outcomes:
      - The first class at the newly expanded sewing school in Goh graduated in February 2026. The Unitarian Community of Victoria, British Columbia, through the Zoie Gardner Overseas Fund (ZGOF), funded the program expansion, an instructor with a business background, and a job coach to help graduates seize sustainable income opportunities.
      - 30 women started in September 2025.
      - 26 graduates are already earning incomes with their new tailoring skills.
      - Job coaching has helped women establish sustainable income-generating businesses.
      - For some, the transformation was profound, progressing from being unable to write their own names to achieving personal and economic independence.
    callout:
      text: For women living in poverty, a sewing machine is more than a tool. It opens the door to skills, income, family support and children’s education.
      attribution: The power of sewing machines in rural villages

    heading: Open Hands *Elementary School*
    body:
      - Since 2018, OHET has provided free education each year for 75 to 85 disadvantaged children from kindergarten through grade 5. For many families, the cost of school supplies would otherwise put education out of reach, leaving children vulnerable to child labour.
      - Through the program, students receive free education, books and school supplies, nutritious food and a welcoming learning environment.
    outcomes:
      - On July 23, 2025, new school supplies and treats were distributed to 80 children while celebrating the eighth year of the OHET School in Dadawan Village.
      - During the year, those children continued receiving free education, school supplies and nutritious food.

    heading: Orphan Support *Program*
    body:
      - Since 2022, OHET has supported children and young adults who have lost one or both parents to remain in school and continue their education. The program assists with educational and living expenses, enabling vulnerable young people to build stable futures despite challenging circumstances.
      - Currently, OHET supports two students attending university.

      - "**Monton** is completing a three-year Bachelor of Education degree and volunteers at the OHET elementary school. After losing her father, continuing her education seemed impossible until OHET provided financial support, including a one-year computer course to strengthen her employment prospects."

  village-elders:
    type: program
    id: village-elders
    number: 4
    heading: Vulnerable Village *Elders* Support Program
    body:
      - Since 2019, OHET has offered support to 45 elderly villagers who have little or no other means of assistance. Throughout the year, participants receive food packages of essential items such as flour, rice, lentils, soybeans, cooking oil, tea, sugar, salt, spices and soap.
      - During winter, blankets are distributed. Helpers assist them in getting the heavy bags home.
    outcomes:
      - In November 2025, OHET distributed food packages and blankets to 45 elderly villagers during a community gathering at the school.

  wash:
    type: program
    id: wash
    number: 5
    heading: Clean Water, Sanitation and *Hygiene*
    body:
      - Since 2018, OHET has improved access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene through a deep well with a hand pump, community toilets and handwashing facilities. These essential resources have reduced disease, improved health, strengthened dignity and ensured reliable access to clean water during periods of increasing drought.
      - Hygiene education reinforces proper handwashing and sanitation practices, while women and girls benefit from greater privacy and safety. Older adults and children no longer need to travel long distances to collect clean water.
    outcomesLead: Multiple infrastructure renovations enhance health
    outcomes:
      - During 2025–2026, OHET upgraded the community toilets, repaired the village hand pump and upgraded the handwashing station in Dadawan. These improvements have strengthened access to safe drinking water, sanitation and hygiene, reducing the spread of disease while improving quality of life.
