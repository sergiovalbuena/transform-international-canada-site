    heading: Completing school is only the *first step*
    body:
      - For many young people in Rwanda, completing school is only the first step toward building a secure future. Limited access to vocational training, financial constraints, and a mismatch between education and labour market needs often leave youth without clear pathways to meaningful employment. Through our partnership with Friends Peace House, Transform International Canada is helping bridge that gap by equipping young people with practical, marketable skills that lead to employment, entrepreneurship, and greater economic independence.
      - While Rwanda continues to experience growth in sectors such as tourism, information technology, and agro-processing, employers continue to report shortages of skilled workers. At the same time, many young people—particularly those from vulnerable households—face barriers to accessing quality vocational education and gaining the practical experience needed to secure employment.

    heading: Friends Peace House and the *Mwana Nshuti* Vocational Training School
    body:
      - Friends Peace House was established in response to the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi and has spent nearly three decades investing in Rwanda's next generation. Its Mwana Nshuti Vocational Training School, founded in 1998, provides practical training in hairdressing, culinary arts, sewing, construction, and auto mechanics. The school combines technical instruction with personal development, helping students gain the confidence, experience, and entrepreneurial skills needed to build sustainable livelihoods.
      - By aligning vocational education with local labour market needs, the Youth Trades Program equips graduates with skills that remain relevant in a changing economy while creating opportunities for long-term financial independence and community development.

  highlights:
    type: text

    eyebrow: Program highlights
    heading: Opportunities that extend *beyond the classroom*
    body:
      - Beyond vocational instruction, the Youth Trades Program creates opportunities that extend well beyond the classroom. Graduates leave with practical, market-relevant skills that improve their employability and earning potential while helping address skilled labour shortages within their communities. Entrepreneurship and financial literacy training encourage self-employment and small business development, creating new sources of income for families and contributing to local economic growth.
      - The program also delivers broader social benefits. Reliable access to nutritious meals improves students' health, concentration, and academic performance, while meaningful employment opportunities reduce vulnerability to poverty and social exclusion. As graduates apply their skills through employment, entrepreneurship, and community projects, they contribute to stronger local services, improved infrastructure, and more resilient communities.
      - By investing in young people today, the Youth Trades Program is helping build a skilled workforce capable of supporting Rwanda's long-term economic growth. More importantly, it is empowering a new generation with the confidence, knowledge, and opportunity to shape their own futures. In doing so, they strengthen the communities they call home.
