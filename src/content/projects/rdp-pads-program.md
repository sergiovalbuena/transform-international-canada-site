    heading: Tackling *period poverty* in rural Malawi
    body:
      - "Through our partnership with Rural Development Partners (RDP), Transform International Canada is helping address one of the leading barriers to young women and girls' education and independence in rural Malawi: period poverty."
      - Many girls and women lack access to safe, affordable menstrual products and reliable information about menstrual health. As a result, menstruation remains one of the leading causes of school absenteeism among girls. Deep-rooted cultural taboos and misinformation also contribute to stigma, discrimination, and exclusion, limiting confidence, health, and participation in daily life.

  approach:
    type: text
    id: approach
    eyebrow: Our approach
    heading: Practical and *community-led*
    body:
      - The Youth Menstrual Hygiene Program responds through a practical, community-led approach that combines health education with sewing skills. Girls and young women learn to produce high-quality reusable menstrual pads from locally available materials while receiving education on reproductive health, hygiene, and personal wellbeing.
      - The program extends beyond sewing clubs for girls across school districts. It also supports local mothers' groups receiving sewing training and gradually fostering their leadership of the initiative. This transfer of knowledge creates local ownership, strengthens community support, and ensures the program continues into future generations.
      - By reducing stigma, encouraging open discussion, and equipping women and girls with practical skills, the program promotes dignity, confidence, and educational opportunity while building sustainable solutions that benefit entire communities.

    heading: Knowledge, skills and *resources*

      - "The Youth Menstrual Hygiene Program equips communities with the knowledge, skills, and resources needed to manage menstrual health independently. The program does this by:"
    list:
      - "**Training girls and women to produce reusable menstrual pads** using locally sourced materials, creating an affordable and sustainable alternative to disposable products while supporting local businesses."
      - "**Establishing school sewing clubs** that provide ongoing access to equipment, materials, and peer support while ensuring future students continue to benefit from the program."
      - "**Partnering with rural schools** to identify participating communities, establish sewing clubs, and integrate menstrual health education into school activities."
      - "**Empowering local mothers' groups** to mentor participants and eventually lead the program. Once trained, these community leaders assume responsibility for teaching sewing skills, maintaining equipment, and managing local distribution."
      - "**Including boys and young men in menstrual health education** to challenge misconceptions, reduce stigma and bullying, encourage empathy, and foster healthier, more supportive school environments."
    after:
      - Together, these activities build local capacity, reduce dependence on outside assistance, and create lasting improvements in menstrual health management.

  outcomes:
    type: text
    id: outcomes
    eyebrow: 2025–2026 program outcomes
    heading: Locally owned systems in *more school districts*
    body:
      - During the July 2025 to June 2026 reporting period, Rural Development Partners, in collaboration with Transform International Canada, expanded the Youth Menstrual Hygiene Program to additional rural school districts. Together, students, teachers, and mothers' groups are creating locally owned systems that enable girls to manage menstruation safely and confidently while remaining engaged in their education.
      - "**Outputs**"
    list:
      - Produced reusable menstrual pads through community sewing groups.
      - Distributed Dignity Kits containing reusable pads, underwear, and storage bags to vulnerable adolescent girls.
      - Supported school-based sewing clubs with equipment, materials, and ongoing training.
      - Trained local mothers' groups to manage program delivery, sewing machine maintenance, material procurement, and local distribution.
      - Educated students through workshops on reproductive health, menstrual hygiene, sexual health, and personal wellbeing.
